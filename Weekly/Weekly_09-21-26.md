## 1. Market Summary

Equity markets posted a split verdict for the week ending September 25, 2026, with large-cap growth indices advancing while small-caps and cyclicals retreated. The S&P 500 gained +0.66% and the Nasdaq led with +1.29%, both driven almost entirely by a powerful Monday rally — the S&P and Nasdaq surged +1.49% and +2.26%, respectively — before giving back ground mid-week and consolidating into Friday's close. The Dow Jones slipped -0.21% and the Russell 2000 fell -1.27%, underscoring that the week's gains were narrow and concentrated in technology and healthcare.

The dominant macro story of the week was the sharp rise in long-end Treasury yields: the 10-year climbed +22 basis points to close at 5.18%, while the 2-year added +11 bps to 4.87%, steepening the 2Y/10Y curve to +31 bps. This yield-curve move inflicted meaningful losses on rate-sensitive sectors — Utilities (XLU, -3.09%), Real Estate (XLRE, -1.87%), and Financials (XLF, -2.16%) — and drove declines in both high-yield (HYG, -1.03%) and investment-grade credit (LQD, -1.74%). In commodities, WTI crude oil suffered its worst weekly performance in recent months, plunging -8.50% to $92.41, while gold and silver also declined as the stronger dollar and rising real yields weighed on precious metals. Natural gas was a notable outlier, surging +10.13% on seasonal and supply dynamics.

Beneath the headline index gains, market breadth remained deeply concerning: only 27.4% of S&P 500 members traded above their 50-day moving average, just 10 stocks hit new 52-week highs versus 12 at new lows, and the advance/decline line — though positive on Friday — masks a week of deteriorating internals. Crypto outperformed, with Bitcoin gaining +3.61% and Ethereum +1.77%, providing a risk-appetite counterpoint to the bond-market anxiety. Overall, the week's narrative is one of a narrow, momentum-driven equity rally straining against a rising yield environment — a tension that will require resolution in the weeks ahead.
