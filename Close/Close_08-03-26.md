## 3. Risks

- **Elevated long-end yields and strong DXY:** The 10-year Treasury yield at 4.75% and DXY at 119.70 represent a dual headwind — higher real borrowing costs suppress valuation multiples for growth equities and a strong dollar compresses earnings for multinationals. If yields move higher from current levels, the equity rally's sustainability becomes increasingly dependent on earnings growth rather than multiple expansion.
- **WTI Crude Oil sharp decline (-5.34%):** A single-session drop of this magnitude in crude oil — on a day when equities rallied — may signal emerging demand weakness or a significant supply event that markets have not yet fully priced into energy-dependent sectors; if the move reflects slowing global industrial activity, it could foreshadow downward revisions to energy-sector earnings and potentially broader cyclical names in coming weeks.
- **Credit market divergence:** HYG (-0.21%) and LQD (-0.13%) declined modestly even as equities rallied sharply, a minor but noteworthy divergence. High-yield spreads and investment-grade credit are often leading indicators of risk appetite; sustained softness in credit alongside an equity rally can be an early warning of fragility, particularly given the 4.75% 10-year yield environment already pressuring debt-servicing capacity across leveraged issuers.
