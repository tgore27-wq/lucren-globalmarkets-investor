## 1. Market Summary

U.S. equity markets closed modestly higher on Tuesday, August 25, with all four major indices posting small gains in a session defined more by its internal divergence than its headline numbers. The S&P 500 added +0.32% to 7,677.28 and the Nasdaq led with +0.66% to 26,151.30, yet under the surface breadth was decisively negative — 294 S&P 500 constituents declined against only 207 advancers — a reminder that index-level calm can mask stock-level stress.

Technology (XLK +0.94%) was the standout sector, with an analyst upgrade of AMD to Strong Buy adding incremental momentum to megacap semi names. Healthcare (XLV +0.34%) provided a second pillar of support, continuing its strong monthly (+7.28%) and three-month (+18.03%) trend. On the other side of the ledger, Energy (XLE -1.66%) bore the brunt of a steep -3.12% decline in WTI crude oil to $82.36 — extending the commodity's weekly loss past -3.0% — while Industrials (XLI -0.34%) slipped further, now down -2.62% on the month and barely positive over three months.

Credit markets offered a reassuring counterpoint: LQD gained +0.64% and HYG advanced +0.28%, suggesting that institutional buyers are not fleeing risk despite the mixed breadth picture. With the VIX closing at a contained 15.45 and the 2Y/10Y spread at a still-positive +47 bps, the macro backdrop remains supportive, but investors should not ignore the soft breadth and the elevated DXY (118.06) as potential friction points heading into the remainder of the week.

## 3. Risks

- **Breadth deterioration contradicts index strength:** With 294 decliners vs. 207 advancers and only 58.8% of S&P 500 stocks above their 50-day MA (below the 60% health threshold), the headline index gains are being driven by a shrinking pool of winners. If leadership continues to narrow, the index becomes increasingly vulnerable to a reversal when those leading names face selling pressure.
- **Elevated DXY (118.06) as a persistent headwind:** A dollar index at 118.06 exerts continuous pressure on US multinational revenue (international sales translate back at a disadvantage), commodity prices in dollar terms, and EM capital flows. Should the DXY remain elevated or move higher, it could weigh on Q3 earnings revisions for globally-exposed companies — a risk not yet fully reflected in S&P 500 monthly gains of +3.56%.
- **Energy sector contagion from crude's slide:** WTI oil falling -3.12% in a single session and -12.28% over three months is not merely a sector-specific story. Sustained crude weakness can pressure Energy-sector credit spreads, reduce capital expenditure across E&P names, and ultimately weigh on Industrials (already -2.62% monthly) through reduced equipment and services demand. Investors with broad Energy or Industrial exposure should reassess downside scenarios if WTI approaches the $78–$80 support range.
