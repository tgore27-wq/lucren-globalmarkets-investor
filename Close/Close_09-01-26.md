## 1. Market Summary

U.S. equities began September on a cautious note, with all four major indices declining as sellers dominated the opening session of the month. The S&P 500 shed -0.71% to close at 7,631.47, the Nasdaq fell -1.03% to 26,099.77, and small-caps bore the brunt of the selling — the Russell 2000 dropped -1.23% to 2,920.13 — reflecting risk-off rotation away from rate-sensitive and growth-oriented segments of the market. Market breadth was decisively negative, with only 158 S&P 500 constituents advancing versus 341 declining, and just 9 new 52-week highs compared to 15 new lows, signaling that today's losses were broadly distributed rather than concentrated in a handful of names. The principal exception to the risk-off theme was the Energy sector, where WTI Crude's +5.95% surge to $90.86 propelled XLE to a +2.04% gain and lifted Natural Gas as well; Technology (XLK +0.44%) was the only other green sector, buoyed by large-cap mega-tech names that provided modest Nasdaq support despite the index's overall decline. Credit markets offered little reassurance, with both HYG (-0.89%) and LQD (-0.93%) declining in tandem with equities, suggesting that the session's weakness was not solely equity-specific but reflected a broader de-risking impulse. With the 10-year yield holding at 4.75%, the dollar near 118.75, and crude oil approaching the psychologically significant $91 level, the stagflationary macro backdrop is becoming an increasingly prominent concern as markets enter the historically challenging month of September.

## 3. Risks

- **Crude oil spike introducing stagflationary pressure:** A +5.95% single-day move in WTI to $90.86, with a 3-month trajectory of +21.86%, risks reigniting inflation expectations at precisely the moment the Fed is weighing its next move. Sustained crude above $90 complicates any near-term Fed pivot narrative and could compress corporate margins, particularly in consumer discretionary and industrials — both of which are already among today's laggards.
- **Deteriorating market breadth and technical structure:** With only 46.8% of S&P 500 members above their 50-day moving average and 65.1% above the 200-day (below the 70% bull-market threshold), the index's headline level is being supported by a narrowing cohort of stocks. This kind of breadth deterioration historically precedes more significant index-level corrections if it is not reversed quickly.
- **Strong dollar and rising long-end yields as a dual headwind:** The DXY at 118.75 and the 10-year yield at 4.75% represent a challenging dual constraint — a strong dollar pressures multinational revenue and EM risk assets, while elevated long yields tighten financial conditions and discount growth equity valuations. The decline in both HYG and LQD today suggests credit markets are beginning to reflect these pressures, which warrants close monitoring for signs of broader stress.
